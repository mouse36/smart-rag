Social Communication Anxiety Therapy (S-CAT) is the philosophy of treatment developed by international expert, Dr. Elisa Shipon- Blum, and implemented at the Selective Mutism Anxiety Research and Treatment Center (SMart Center) www.selectivemutismcenter.org.
社交焦虑治疗（S-CAT）是国际专家Elisa Shipon-Blum博士开发的治疗理念，并在选择性焦虑研究和治疗中心（SMart Center）实施。www.selectivemutismcenter.org


S-CAT is based on the concept that Selective Mutism is a social communication anxiety disorder that is more than just not speaking.
S-CAT基于这样一个概念，即选择性缄默症是一种社交沟通焦虑症，不仅仅是不说话。


Dr. Shipon-Blum has created the SM-Stages of Social Communication Comfort Scale © that describes the various stages of social communication that are possible for a child suffering from Selective Mutism. The Social Communication Bridge © illustrates this concept in a visual form.
Shipon-Blum博士创造了社交沟通舒适量表的SM阶段，该阶段描述了一个孩子患有选择性缄默症可能的社会交流的各个阶段。社交沟通桥©以视觉的形式阐释了这个概念。


Children suffering from Selective Mutism (SM) CHANGE their level of social communication based on the setting and expectations from others WITHIN a setting. Therefore social comfort and communication will change from SETTING to SETTING and PERSON TO PERSON.
患有选择性互惠（SM）的儿童根据其他人的设置和期望，改变他们的社交沟通水平。因此，社会的舒适度和沟通将随设置改变和人员的变化而改变。


For example, a child may be 'chatting up a storm' with their friend or perhaps family member in ONE setting, yet see that SAME person in another setting (such as at school or perhaps at a family function) and the child may have difficulty socially engaging, communicating nonverbally and perhaps the child cannot communicate at all!
例如，一个孩子可能与他们的朋友或家庭成员在一个环境中“喋喋不休”，但在另一个环境中（例如在学校或家庭集会中）看到同一个人时，该孩子却可能有困难与之亲近，用非语言沟通，甚至也许根本无法沟通！


For some children, they appear VERY comfortable and MUTISM is the most noted symptom. Meaning, they ARE able to engage and have astute nonverbal skills (professional mimes!) in MOST, if not all settings. These children are STUCK in the nonverbal stage of communication (stage 1) and suffer from a subtype of SM called: SPEECH PHOBIA.
对于一些孩子来说，他们看起来很舒服，缄默是最引人注目的症状。意思是说，即使不是全部情况下，他们也能够在大多数情况下参与并具有敏锐的非语言技能（专业哑剧！）。这些孩子被困在非言语交际阶段（第1阶段），患有SM的亚型称为：言语恐惧症。

Selective Mutism is SO much more than NOT speaking.
选择性缄默症不仅仅是不说话。


‘Not speaking' merely touches on the surface of our children! A complete understanding of the child is necessary to develop an appropriate treatment plan (For home and in the Real World) as well as in school by developing accommodations/interventions (i.e. IEP or 504 plan).
“不说话”只是触及我们孩子的表面！通过开发支持/干预措施（即IEP或504计划），需要对孩子有一个全面的了解，以制定适当的治疗计划（适用于家庭和现实世界以及学校）。


According to Dr. Shipon-Blum's work, after a complete evaluation (consisting of various assessment forms-parent/teacher; parent and child interview), treatment needs to address three key questions.
根据Shipon-Blum博士的工作，经过完整的评估（包括各种评估表格 - 家长/老师，父母和孩子的面谈）后，治疗需要解决三个关键问题。


--WHY did a child develop SM? (Influencing, precipitating and maintaining factors)
- 为什么孩子患上SM？ （影响，促成和保持因素）


--WHY does Selective Mutism persist despite being in active treatment and parent/teacher awareness?
- 尽管积极治疗加上家长/老师的认知，为什么选择性缄默症仍然存在？


And finally, --WHAT can be done at home, the real world and within school to help the child build the coping skills and overcome their social communication challenges?
最后， - 在家里，现实世界和学校内可以做些什么来帮助孩子建立应对技能并克服他们的社交沟通挑战？


To HELP a child suffering in silence an understanding of which stage the child is IN during particular social encounters. The Social Communication Anxiety Inventory (SCAI) can be used to determine the stage of social communication.
Treatment is THEN developed via the WHOLE child approach where, under the direction of the outside treatment professional, the child, parents and school personnel work together. Dr. Shipon-Blum emphasizes that although anxiety lowering is key it is often NOT enough, especially as children age. Over time, many children with Selective Mutism no longer feel 'anxious' but mutism and often lack of proper social engagement continues to exist in select settings.
Children with SM need strategies/interventions to progress from nonverbal to spoken communication. This is the TRANSITIONAL stage of communication and interestingly enough, It is this aspect that is often missing from most treatment plans. In other words, HOW do you help a child progress from NONVERBAL to verbal communication?
帮助一个沉默的孩子了解孩子在特定的社交场合中处于哪个阶段。社交沟通焦虑量表（SCAI）可以用来确定社交沟通的阶段。
治疗是通过整体儿童方法开发的，在外部治疗专业人员的指导下，儿童，父母和学校人员一起工作。 Shipon-Blum博士强调，尽管降低焦虑是关键，但这往往是不够的，特别是随着儿童年龄的增长。随着时间的推移，许多具有选择性互动的儿童不再感到“焦虑”，而是在特定环境中继续存在缄默，并且缺乏适当的社交参与。
SM患儿需要策略/干预，从非言语交流到口头交流。这是过渡阶段的交流，有趣的是，这是大多数治疗计划经常缺失的方面。换句话说，你如何帮助孩子从非言语交流进步到言语交流？

Quite frankly, time in the therapy office is simply NOT enough. The office setting is used to help prepare the child for the outside world. Strategies and interventions are developed based on WHERE the child is on the SOCIAL COMMUNICATION BRIDGE and are meant to be a desensitizing method as well as a vehicle to UNLEARN conditioned behavior and to develop coping skills to combat social communication anxiety.
坦率地说，在治疗办公室的时间是不够的。办公室场景是用于帮助孩子为外部世界做好准备的。策略和干预是基于孩子在社交沟通桥上的哪个位置开发的，并且旨在成为脱敏方法以及对忘记条件行为的运用和开发应对技能以对抗社交沟通焦虑。


S-CAT incorporates anxiety lowering techniques, methods to build self-esteem and strategies/interventions to help with social comfort and communication progression, such as 'Bridging' from shut down to nonverbal communication and then TRANSITIONING into spoken communication via verbal intermediaries, ritual sound shaping and possibly the use of augmentative devices etc.
S-CAT结合了焦虑降低技术，建立自尊的方法和策略/干预措施，以帮助社交舒适和交流进展，如从关闭到非语言交流的“桥梁”，然后通过言语中介，仪式声音塑造，可能的话使用强化设备等，过渡到口语交流。


The KEY concept that children with SM need to understand, feel in control and have choice in their treatment (age dependent) are a critical component of S-CAT.
S-CAT provides CHOICE to the child and helps to transfer the child's NEED for control INTO the strategies and interventions!
一个关键概念是在治疗中SM患儿需要了解，感觉掌控并有选择权（依年龄而定）。这是S-CAT的关键组成部分。
S-CAT为孩子提供“选择”，帮助将孩子的掌控需求转移到策略和干预中去！


Therefore GAMES and GOALS (based on age) via the use of ritualistic and controlled methods (I.e., use of strategy charts) are used to help develop social comfort and progress into speech.
因此，通过使用仪式和控制方法（即使用策略图表），游戏和目标（基于年龄）被用于帮助发展社交舒适度和进入言语进程。


Silent goals (environmental changes; parent education, school staff education, school based accommodations/interventions) and active goals (child directed goals based on CHOICE and CONTROL) are used within the S-CAT program.
在S-CAT项目中使用无声目标（环境变化;家长教育，学校教职工教育，在校支持/干预）和积极目标（基于选择和控制的儿童指导的目标）。


Every child is different and therefore an individualized treatment plan needs to be developed that incorporates HOME (parent education, environmental changes), Addressing the child's unique needs and SCHOOL (teacher education, accommodations/interventions).
每个孩子都是不同的，因此需要开发包含家庭（家长教育，环境变化），满足孩子独特需求和学校（师资教育，支持/干预）的个性化治疗计划。


Therefore, by lowering anxiety, increasing self-esteem as well as increasing communication and social confidence within a variety of REAL WORLD settings, the child suffering in silence will develop necessary coping skills to enable for proper social, emotional and academic functioning.


因此，通过降低焦虑，增强自尊，以及在各种真实世界环境中增加沟通和社交信心，饱受沉默苦恼的孩子将培养必要的应对技能，以实现适当的社交，情感和学术功能。

SELECTIVE MUTISM -STAGES OF SOCIAL COMMUNICATION COMFORT SCALE
©
选择性缄默症-社会交往舒适度的标尺


NON-COMMUNICATIVE 
-neither non-verbal nor verbal. NO social engagement.
STAGE 0 - NO Responding, NO initiating
Child stands motionless (stiff body language), expressionless, averts eye gaze, appears ‘frozen,’ MUTE OR Seemingly IGNORES person while interacting or speaking to other(s). MUTE towards others
For communication to occur, Social Engagement must occur
无交流
- 即无非语言交流又无口头交流。不参与社交活动
阶段0  - 没有响应，没有主动交流
儿童静止不动（僵硬的肢体语言），无表情，避免眼睛注视，出现“冻结”，静音或看似忽视某人而在与其他人交流或谈话。对其他人无动于衷。
为了进行交流，社交活动必须发生






COMMUNICATIVE
(Nonverbal and/or Verbal*) 
*TO ADVANCE FROM ONE STAGE OF COMMUNICATION TO THE NEXT,
INCREASING SOCIAL COMFORT NEEDS TO OCCUR.
STAGE 1 - Nonverbal Communication: (NV) 1A Responding -pointing, nodding, writing, sign language, gesturing, use of ‘objects’ (e.g. whistles, bells, Non-voice augmentative device (e.g. communication boards/cards, symbols, photos)
1B Initiating -getting someone’s attention via pointing, gesturing, writing, use of ‘objects’ to get attention (e.g. whistles, bells, Non-voice augmentative device (e.g. communication boards/cards, symbols, photos)
有交流 
（非语言和/或口头*）
*从一个交流阶段前进到下一个交流阶段，需要增加社会舒适度。

阶段1  - 非语言沟通：（NV）
1A响应 - 指向，点头，写作，手语，手势，使用'物体'（例如哨子，钟声，非语音增强设备（例如通讯板/卡片，符号，照片）


STAGE 2 - Transition into Verbal Communication (TV)
2A Responding -Via any sounds, (e.g. grunts, animal sounds, letter sounds, moans, etc.): Verbal
Intermediary or Whisper Buddy; Augmentative Device with sound, (e.g. simple message switch, multiple voice message device, tape recorder, video, etc.)
2B Initiating -Getting someone’s attention via any sounds, (e.g. grunts, animal sounds, letter sounds, moans., etc.): Verbal Intermediary or Whisper Buddy; Augmentative Device with sound, (e.g. , simple message switch, multiple voice message device ,tape recorder, video, etc)
第2阶段 - 过渡到言语交流（TV）
2A响应 - 发出任何声音（例如咕噜声，动物声音，字母声音，呻吟声等）：
口头表达中介或耳语伙伴;具有声音的增强装置（例如，简单消息切换器，多语音消息装置，磁带录音机，视频等）
2B起始交流 - 通过任何声音（例如咕噜声，动物声音，字母声音，呻吟声等）引起别人的注意：言语中介或耳语伙伴;具有声音的增强装置（例如，简单消息切换器，多语音消息装置，磁带录音机，视频等）


STAGE 3 - Verbal Communication (VC)
3A Responding – Approximate speech/direct speech (e.g. altered or made-up language, baby
talk, reading/rehearsing script, soft whispering, speaking)
3B Initiating - Approximate speech/direct speech (e.g. altered or made-up language, baby talk,
reading/rehearsing script, soft whispering, speaking)
第3阶段 - 言语交流（VC）
3A回应 - 大概的发言/直接发言（例如改变或编造的语言，婴儿谈话，阅读/排练脚本，轻声低语，说话）
3B起始交流 - 近似言语/直接言语（例如改变或编造的语言，婴儿说话，阅读/排练脚本，轻声低语，说话）

This book is designed to help parents, teachers and therapists’ support and guide children who have Selective Mutism, teaching them to cope with the stressful feelings they experience in school. With these tactics, along with proper therapy, there is every reason to believe that Selectively Mute children can succeed in school, beat their anxiety disorder, and enjoy their school days to the fullest!
这本书旨在帮助家长，老师和治疗师的支持和指导有选择性的互动的孩子，教他们应付他们在学校所经历的压力感受。有了这些策略，加上适当的治疗，我们有理由相信选择性静音儿童可以在学校取得成功，打破他们的焦虑症，并尽情享受他们的上学时间！


For the majority of children, going to school is filled with exciting anticipation and joy...seeing old friends, making new friends, learning tons of new stuff, being out of the house and having fun...but for the Selectively Mute child, back-to-school can be terrifying!
对于大多数孩子来说，去学校充满了令人兴奋的期待和欢乐......看到老朋友，结交新朋友，学习大量新东西，不在家里玩乐......但对于选择性缄默的孩子，回校可能会很可怕！


As most of us already know, well over 90% of Selectively Mute children have social phobia in addition to Selective Mutism. These children actually feel like they are on stage almost every minute of the day! Just imagine what this is like...pure fear from morning until afternoon. These children are so fearful that they literally cannot speak!
正如我们大多数人已经知道的那样，超过90％的SM儿童除了选择性缄默症之外还有社交恐惧症。这些孩子们实际上觉得他们几乎每天的每一分钟都在舞台上！想象一下什么感觉......，从早晨到下午，这就是纯粹的恐惧。这些孩子非常害怕，他们几乎不会说话！


The anxiety and tension that results from this can be very debilitating for most children, day in and day out. Tension, anxiety, fearfulness, and apprehension are just a few of the many emotions that SM children experience. Many, if not all, eventually have significant physical symptoms that accompany their anxiety; headaches, stomachaches, dizziness, nausea, diarrhea and joint pains are a few examples. In addition, SM children often manifest symptoms of other anxiety disorders, such as fears, phobias, generalized worries, panic and obsessive/compulsive symptoms like hoarding. The array of anxious feelings that the typical SM child feels is well beyond the obvious ‘lack of speaking.’
由此产生的焦虑和紧张可能会使大多数儿童日复一日地疲惫不堪。紧张，焦虑，恐惧和忧虑只是SM儿童经历的许多情绪中的一小部分。许多人（如果不是全部的话）最终会有显著的身体症状伴随着焦虑症状;一些例子是头痛，胃痛，头晕，恶心，腹泻和关节痛。此外，SM儿童常常表现出其他焦虑症的症状，如恐惧，恐惧症，全身忧虑，恐慌和囤积等强迫症状。典型的SM小孩所感受到的焦虑情绪远远超出了明显的“缺乏说话”。


These children are not lying, pretending, manipulating, or attempting to seek attention; they are actually feeling these symptoms. As our children get older, they often feel alone and withdrawn, believing they are the only ones experiencing these feelings.

建议并计划举办木偶戏，打扮和虚拟游戏等活动。令人惊讶的是，通过使用虚假的声音或者像其他人一样“表演”，SM儿童通常能够很好地沟通（甚至可以口头表达）。

在夏季，课余时间或周末，带将和你孩子同班的小孩进入学校。

Allow your child to play as much as possible outside in the school playground or playing field. The goal is to desensitize your child to the school playground. Bring friends along. These should be the same friends with whom your child is having play dates!
Try to use these tips as examples, and modify them for your child. Obviously these are not ‘miracle cures.’ However, building self-confidence and easing stress, together with proper therapy, love and encouragement, are wonderful building blocks for your child’s overall success in school and in overcoming Selective Mutism!

29 ASK THE DOC Frequently Asked Questions
(1)
My son seems to like school, but when we go in the morning, he tends to cling to me and has a blank, expressionless face. He looks like a statue! Why does this happen? What you are describing is the typical ‘SM look.’ Reasons for this are due to ‘anxiety’ and the effects anxiety has on our children’s bodies. When anxious, our children get the ‘fear’ look, similar to a frightened animal in the wild. Certain areas of the brain are stimulated when in anxious situations, and this causes a variety of responses; one of which is muscle stiffening. Since the face is lined with muscle, our children’s blank look is a direct reflection of skeletal muscle tension. This has nothing to do with their liking of school or any other situation, just a manifestation of anxiety
问医生：常见问题
（1）
我的儿子似乎喜欢上学，但是当我们在早上去的时候，他倾向于抱着我，并且有一张空白的，毫无表情的脸。他看起来像一个雕像！为什么会发生？你所描述的是典型的“SM平板脸”，其原因是由于“焦虑”以及焦虑对我们孩子身体的影响。当焦虑时，我们的孩子会看到“恐惧”，就像野外受惊的动物一样。在焦虑的情况下，大脑的某些区域会受到刺激，这会引起各种反应;其中之一是肌肉僵硬。由于面部肌肉衬里，我们孩子的空白外观是骨骼肌张力的直接反映。这与他们是否喜欢学校或任何其他情况无关，只是焦虑的表现。


(2)
The frozen appearance (as described in (1) above) was present at the beginning of the school year and right after holiday breaks and long weekends. Seems that as the spring is approaching, I am seeing less and less of that ‘look.’ Does this mean anxiety is less? Yes. Our children tend to be most anxious during transition times (start of new school year or returning back to school after being home for a holiday or illness). Interestingly enough, as the school year progresses and our children become more comfortable with their teachers, classmates and school environment, that ‘fearful’ frozen-look dissipates. You will also notice that as the ‘look’ dissipates our children tend to communicate better. This might be in the form of nonverbal communication comfort, but nevertheless, communication often begins to occur. It should be noted that as the school year progresses and the look goes away, unless the child is involved in treatment to help build communication skills, that ‘look’ may reappear at the beginning of the next school year, and the same scenario will reoccur.
（2）
冻结的外观（如上面（1）中所述）出现在学年开始时和节假日休息和长周末。似乎随着春天的临近，我看到的越来越少，看起来像这样。这是否意味着焦虑更少？是。我们的孩子在过渡时期（新学年开始或在家中度假或生病后返校）往往是最焦虑的。有趣的是，随着学年的进展，我们的孩子们对老师，同学和学校环境变得更加舒适，这种'可怕'的冰冻外观消失了。你也会注意到，随着'平板脸'的消散，我们的孩子往往会更好地沟通。这可能是非言语沟通舒适的形式，但是沟通往往开始发生。应该注意的是，随着学年的进展和目光消失，除非孩子参与治疗以帮助建立沟通技巧，那么'平板脸'可能会在下一学年开始时重新出现，并且同样的情况会再次出现。


(3)
We have four SM children in our school district. The younger elementary students seem to ‘shut down’ and get that blank, frozen look, yet the older SM students are mute but appear more relaxed in school. Is this common? From working with Selectively Mute children of all ages, I have noticed that the ‘frozen-look’ is much more common in younger children. Although older children and teens can portray that anxious look, it certainly is not nearly as common. Reasons for this are that as time goes on, our children learn to adjust and feel more comfortable in social environments, such as school. From years of being in a social environment, they develop methods of coping. However if they are still mute, they have not developed the coping skills to be able to communicate effectively.
（3）
我们学区有四名SM儿童。年龄小的小学生似乎很“关闭”，并有张空白，冷冻的平板脸，然而年长的SM学生却是哑巴，但在学校看起来更放松。这是常见的吗？
从与所有年龄段的选择性缄默症儿童合作以来，我注意到“冰冻外观”在年幼的儿童中更常见。虽然年龄较大的孩子和青少年可以表现出焦虑的外表，但它的确不是那么常见。这是因为随着时间的推移，我们的孩子在学校等社交环境中学习调整并感觉更舒适。从处于社会环境的多年开始，他们开发了应对方法。但是，如果他们仍然缄默，他们还没有开发出应对技能，以便能够有效沟通。


(4)
My daughter can speak easily to her best friend everywhere but school. The second my daughter is in school, she cannot even whisper to her friend. My daughter tells me that she just ‘can’t get the words out’ in school. Is this common and what can I do to help? This scenario is very common. In fact, for the majority of young SM children this happens quite often. Your child’s anxiety is heightened within the school environment. Her anxiety is stifling and crippling her to the point that she is mute with her friend and unable to speak. To build verbal comfort with her friend in school, I suggest beginning with a play date at home, where verbalization is occurring freely, and then transferring the play date to the school (start off on the playground or grounds, then into the classroom). Ideally this should eventually occur with few others around. Chances are that since your child has been speaking to her friend easily, speaking will continue since there are minimal outside pressures. I suggest doing this often and consistently so that your child can ‘see’ herself as a speaker with her friend within school. As this scenario becomes easier and easier for your child, your treating professional should help your child progress to whispering and communicating during the school session.
（4）
我的女儿可以轻松地和她最好的朋友讲话，除了学校以外。第二个女儿在学校，她甚至不能对她的朋友耳语。我的女儿告诉我，她在学校“无法说出”。这是常见的吗？我可以做些什么来帮助？这种情况非常普遍。事实上，对于大多数年小的SM儿童来说，这种情况经常发生。在学校环境中，你的孩子的焦虑情绪升高。她的焦虑使她感到窒息，并使她陷入了与她的朋友无话并且无法说话的地步。为了与她的朋友在学校建立口语舒适感，我建议先在家里玩游戏，在那里自由发表言语，然后把游戏约会转移到学校（从操场或校园场地开始，然后进入教室）。理想情况下，这应该最终与周围的其他几个人有舒适感。由于你的孩子很容易与她的朋友说话，她们开学后会继续说下去， 因为外部压力很小。我建议你经常这样做，始终如一，以便你的孩子能够在校内“看待”自己作为演讲者来与她的朋友相处。随着这种情况对你的孩子变得越来越容易，你的治疗专业人员应该帮助你的孩子在学校会话期间进行耳语和大声交流。


(5)
My son’s friend is upset with him because my son won’t talk to him in school. He and other children are always trying to get my son to talk in school. How can I help? First off, your son CAN’T talk in school; it is not that he WON’T talk in school. Anxiety is high and your son becomes communicatively impaired. I suggest talking to your son first. Let him know that you understand. Let him know that although speaking is hard and can be scary at times, everyone has something that is difficult. I suggest making a list with your son of things he can do well and easily. Talk about this openly and let your son express his feelings to you. Then, once he is comfortable, he can either approach his friend/s (if he is old enough and feels comfortable) or you can talk with the other child and your son together. Explain to your son’s friend that ‘talking’ in school is hard right now, and that your son’s inability to talk has nothing to do with his friendship and liking towards him or other friends. Ask your son’s friend to think about something that is hard for him, and then compare that to your son’s inability to get the words out easily in school. With regard to other children in the class; talk with your child’s teacher about this same concept. There is a FAQ in the book “The Ideal Classroom Setting for the Selectively Mute Child” that deals specifically with ways to handle the curious ones in the class that do not understand an SM child’s silence. Having other children identify with feelings of their own that can compare to how an SM child may feel is an excellent way to get the point across and explain why children with SM are unable to speak at times.
（5）
我儿子的朋友对他感到不满，因为我的儿子不会在学校跟他说话。他和其他孩子总是试图让我的儿子在学校说话。我该怎么帮忙？首先，你的儿子不能在学校说话;并不是说他不愿在学校说话。焦虑很高，你的儿子变得沟通障碍。我建议先和你儿子说话。让他知道你理解。让他知道，尽管说话很难并且有时可能很吓人，但每个人都有困难。我建议你和他的儿子一起列出他能做得很好而且容易做的事情。公开谈论这个，让你的儿子向你表达他的感受。然后，一旦他感到舒服，他可以接近他的朋友（如果他已经足够大并且感觉舒服），或者你可以和另一个孩子和你的儿子一起聊天。向你儿子的朋友解释，现在在学校“说话”很难，你儿子无法说话与他的友谊和对他或其他朋友的喜爱无关。让你儿子的朋友思考一些对他来说很难的事情，然后把它与你儿子在学校里无法轻易搞清楚。关于班上其他孩子;和你孩子的老师谈论同样的概念。 “选择性静音儿童的理想课堂设置”一书中有一个常见问题解答，专门讨论如何处理课堂中不了解SM儿童沉默的好奇课程。让其他孩子认同自己的感受可以与SM孩子的感受相比，这是一个很好的方式来解决问题并解释为什么SM儿童有时无法说话。


(6)
My child says she does not speak at school because she doesn't like the sound of her voice. Yet, she talks, sings and yells quite confidently at home. In fact, she often sings at the top of her voice in the grocery store (until someone approaches her then she shuts down). Is it common for SM children to say that they do not like their voice? Interestingly enough, there are some SM children who say this very same thing... that they do not like the sound of their voice. Reasons for this vary from truly not liking their voice, having been told they ‘talk funny’ or are ‘hard to understand’ to just coming up with a reason as to why they are mute. A suggestion would be to try speech therapy. Working with a speech and language pathologist can help build verbal confidence and may help your child tremendously! Along with the speech therapy, working with a treating professional who can help with other anxiety lowering techniques, such as practicing verbalization in the school with you before and after school, having frequent play dates with children from the class, bringing those kids into class before and after school, etc....are all examples of how to help your SM child feel more socially confident and comfortable.
（6）
我的孩子说她不在学校说话，因为她不喜欢她的声音。然而，她会说话，在家里非常自信地唱歌和大叫。事实上，她经常在杂货店里用最大声唱歌（直到有人接近她，然后她闭嘴）。 SM儿童说他们不喜欢他们的声音很常见吗？有趣的是，还有一些SM孩子说同样的话......即他们不喜欢他们的声音。原因有很多，从真正的不喜欢他们的声音，被告知他们“说话可笑”或“难以理解”，到编出他们为什么哑巴的原因。一个建议是尝试言语治疗。与言语和语言病理学家一起工作可以帮助建立口头信心，并可能极大地帮助您的孩子！与言语治疗一起，与能够帮助其他焦虑降低技巧的治疗专业人士一起工作，例如在上学前后与你一起在学校练习口头表达，与班上的孩子频繁玩游戏，带这些孩子上课和放学等......都是如何帮助你的SM孩子感受到更多社交自信和舒适的例子。


(7)
My son has been invited to a birthday party for one of his school friends at a nearby bowling alley. He indicates that he wants to go, but experience tells me that the day will come and my son will not want to go. And, if we do go, he will stand in the corner alone unable to interact with others. Then, as the party is over, he will start interacting with his friends. I am not sure how to handle this situation. This is such a common occurrence for our SM children. First off, your child WANTS to go to the party. Since the majority of SM children have social anxiety symptoms, the day of the party often elicits anxious feelings at the thought of interacting in a social setting. Since our children need a very long ‘warm-up’ period it often takes the entire duration of some parties for some of our children to feel less anxious and ready to interact. This is typical. Suggestions would be to: - Far in advance, talk to your child about the events and location of the party. If not too far, perhaps visit the party location. Perhaps plan on bowling one afternoon or weekend prior to the party! - The day of the party, arrive very early before the majority of the children arrive. Walking into a crowded room is difficult. Minimizing this as much as possible will only help your child. Plus, since ‘warm- up’ time takes a while, the earlier it starts, the better! - Bring along a friend! Nothing like going somewhere with a buddy. ☺ - If, on the day of the party, you do all of the above and your son is still clingy and mute, suggestions would be to just support your child, stay close by and praise them for their brave efforts of going to the party.
（7）
我的儿子被邀请参加他在附近保龄球馆的一个学校朋友的生日聚会。他表示他想去，但经验告诉我，那一天会到来，我的儿子也不想去。而且，如果我们离开，他将独自站在角落，无法与他人互动。然后，当聚会结束时，他将开始与他的朋友互动。我不知道如何处理这种情况。这对我们的SM儿童来说是如此常见。首先，你的孩子要去参加聚会。由于大多数SM儿童都有社交焦虑症状，因此在社交场合互动的思想中，聚会的日子常常​​引发焦虑情绪。由于我们的孩子需要一个很长的“热身”时期，我们的一些孩子经常需要整个过程的一段时间来减少焦虑并准备好互动。这是典型的。建议： - 提前，与您的孩子讨论聚会的事件和地点。如果不是太远，也许可以访问派对地点。也许计划在聚会前的一个下午或周末打保龄球！ - 聚会的日子，在大多数孩子到来之前很早就到达。走进拥挤的房间很困难。尽可能减少这种情况只会对您的孩子有所帮助。另外，由于“预热”时间需要一段时间，因此启动越早越好！ - 带朋友去！没什么能和与哥们一起去某个地方相比。 ☺ - 如果在派对当天，你做了以上所有事情并且你的儿子仍然保持粘人和不说话，那么建议就是支持你的孩子，靠近并赞美他们为参加聚会所做的勇敢努力。


(8)
My 7 yr old daughter has told us that she does not want to speak and is not going to speak until she is in college. Isn’t this oppositional behavior? At first sight, this most certainly seems like the words of an oppositional child. However, having worked with so many SM children, I have come to realize that SM children are not oppositional with their mutism. What happens is that when a child is mute and unable to speak, he/she develops maladaptive methods of coping. Avoidance and denial tend to prevail if the child does not understand why, unlike everyone else; she cannot get the words out. It is easier to state what this child has stated, than to confront something that is completely perplexing to her....mutism. This child does not understand WHY she does not speak, nor does she have any clue HOW to figure out her feelings. She is in complete denial. She needs to work with a treating professional who can help her begin to acknowledge and assess her feelings. Once this occurs, then she is ready to begin treatment.
（8）
我7岁的女儿告诉我们，她不想说话，且直到她上大学时才会要说话。这不是对立的行为吗？乍一看，这当然似乎是一个对立的孩子的话。然而，在与许多SM儿童合作过后，我开始意识到SM儿童不会与他们的缄默态度相抗衡。发生什么事是，当一个孩子哑巴而不能说话时，他/她会发展畸的方法来应对。如果孩子不理解为什么，与其他人不同，她无法把话说出来。说出这个孩子所说的话比说出一些完全令她困惑的东西更容易......缄默。这个孩子不明白她为什么不说话，也不知道如何弄清她的感受。她完全否认。她需要与一位能帮助她开始承认和评估她的感受的专业治疗师合作。一旦发生这种情况，她就可以开始治疗了。


(9)
My child has recently been diagnosed with Selective Mutism. What type of professional can help her? What is important is finding someone who UNDERSTANDS; whether this is a psychologist, social worker, speech pathologist, physician, etc. In other words, as long the treating professional understands the dynamics of Selective Mutism and is able to develop a treatment plan based on her unique situation, then you should be in excellent hands! The team approach is also a must. In other words, coordinating home and school is KEY e.g., working on ways to build communication and social comfort begins with forming friendships with other children. While you are having play dates with specific children, the school is arranging specific social situations involving that child and your child. 如果我的孩子抱怨肚子痛等，我该如何判断我的孩子是否真的患病或者症状是焦虑诱发的（即使没有明显的焦虑原因）？你清楚地看到，'缄默'不是我们孩子特征化的唯一症状。身体症状，如肚子疼，头痛，关节痛和恶心是我们的一些孩子表现出焦虑的另一种方式。这些症状通常表现在处于压力或潜在压力的时候，例如在上学前，派对，与父母分离，展示演说或做报告当天或在其他社交场合期间。其实，对你的孩子来说，这些症状感觉就像他/她“真的”有病。不同之处在于，在焦虑相关症状的情况下，'我感觉不好'通常在压力或不安全感的时候出现。而且，焦虑症状通常会来的快去的也快。换句话说，我们的孩子可能会抱怨肚子疼，但在吃披萨和冰淇淋又没事了，或者可以说他们的腿疼，但却跑下台阶迎接他们在门口的爸爸！真正的疾病通常伴随着其他症状，例如发烧，喉咙痛，疲倦，低能量，嗜睡。如果您有任何怀疑，建议将与您孩子的医生核对。此外，支持你的孩子的生病感受。让他们知道你理解并向他们解释为什么他们可能感觉“不太好”。孩子们通常不会在感觉不舒服和某些事件之间建立联系。他们确实感到不适。帮助他们理解并提供支持只是帮助缓解焦虑症状的另一种方式。

My daughter is in Grade 1. She never spoke in pre-school and still does not speak in school. My husband still believes that she will "grow out of it" and consequently I do not have his support when I have to meet with the school to make accommodations for my daughter. How can I get the school to take ME seriously, if my own husband does not understand? You are witnessing the reality of the situation. SM is not yet a household term. Teachers, treating professionals, family and friends may not understand at all. You are seeing this first hand. You therefore need to become as educated as possible by learning as much as you can about Selective Mutism. Make time for you and your husband to spend time together to talk. Let him know how important this meeting between the two of you is. Be confident in your approach, having pre-printed information to validate your concerns and present material calmly is KEY. Approach your husband in a non-confrontational manner. The calmer you are, the more your words will impact. Let your husband know that children with SM do not just ‘outgrow their silence.’ You can present evidence of this by showing him letters from older teens and adults whose parents thought the same thing. Many of these people were not helped and suffer the consequences of untreated anxiety. Let him know that your child is suffering when in various social settings. I suggest that after your meeting and you have alerted him to the ‘signs’ of a child suffering in silence, you then take him to school, a birthday party or other social event where he can SEE your child anxious, mute and having difficulty initiating play and interacting with others. This will be painful for him and you to see, but unless your husband can SEE what you are talking about, it will be difficult for him to begin to understand. Point this behavior out to him first hand. Take him to as many social outings as possible. Having spoken to many parents, this approach (EDUCATION first, then SHOWING the child in anxious settings) has helped many dads see the situation from a different perspective. In regard to your meeting with the school, please let your husband know how important this is for your child; that you need his support here. Go to the meeting prepared; be confident, take pre-printed information and state the approach that should be taken in the school. Most schools are very willing to learn (if they don’t know already) and will appreciate the information you are presenting them with. Since SM is anything but rare, chances are they will be applying this information to another SM student sooner or later.

我的女儿在1年级。她从未在学前班讲过话，并且仍然在学校没有讲话。我的丈夫仍然认为她会“长大自愈”，因此当我不得不与学校见面为我女儿寻求帮助时，我没有得到他的支持。如果我丈夫不理解，怎么能让学校认真对待我？你正在目睹局势的现实。SM还不是一个家喻户晓的术语。教师，对待专业人士，家人和朋友可能根本不了解。你亲眼看到了这一点。因此，你需要通过尽可能多地学习选择性缄默来成为受过教育的人。让你和你的丈夫花时间花时间在一起聊天。让他知道你们俩之间的这次会晤有多重要。对您的方法充满信心，拥有预先印制的信息以验证您的顾虑并冷静地呈现材料是关键。以非对抗的方式接近你的丈夫。你越平静，你的言语就会越多。让你的丈夫知道SM的孩子不只是“长大了他们的沉默。”你可以通过向他们展示他们的父母认为同样的年长的青少年和成年人的信件来证明这一点。这些人中的许多人没有得到帮助，并且遭受未经治疗的焦虑的后果。让他知道你的孩子在各种社交环境中都在受苦。我建议你在见面后告诉他一个孩子沉默的“迹象”，然后带他去学校，生日聚会或其他社交活动，在那里他可以看到你的孩子焦虑，沉默和难以启动玩耍和与他人互动。这对他和你来说都是痛苦的，但除非你的丈夫能够看到你在说什么，否则他很难开始理解。第一手将这种行为指向他。带他去尽可能多的社交活动。在与许多家长交谈之后，这种方法（教育第一，然后在焦虑的环境中显示孩子）已经帮助许多父亲从不同的角度看待这种情况。关于你与学校的会面，请让你的丈夫知道这对你的孩子有多重要;你需要他的支持。去准备会议;要自信，采取预先印制的信息并说明应该在学校采取的方法。大多数学校都非常愿意学习（如果他们不知道的话）并且会欣赏你提供给他们的信息。由于SM很少见，很可能他们迟早会将此信息应用于另一名SM学生。

When I spend time in my daughter’s classroom, the other children have been questioning why ‘Sally’ does not speak and/or what does her voice sound like? My daughter is making progress but I can tell she is very uncomfortable about others asking these questions. Any suggestions on how I should handle these innocent yet curious questions? This situation is all too common. Actually, honesty is truly the best policy. First, talk to your daughter about these questions. By talking about the questions and discussing responses, you are preparing her for these situations that will inevitably exist. Preparation = feelings of control = less anxiety. When a child asks if 'Sally' speaks, advice is to confidently state, "Yes, Sally speaks quite a bit when she is home and other places. Speaking in school is hard for Sally." When the curious questioners ask, "WHY is talking hard?" mention that "everyone has something that makes them feel scared; perhaps it is the dark, monsters, high buildings, etc. Sally is scared to talk at times, but she is working on it, and feeling less scared lately. Just being her friend is a great way to help her feel less scared." Interestingly enough, children are usually quite satisfied with 'simple' responses. When others ask about the sound of 'Sally's' voice, just say, “Sally talks like all the other kids - like a typical girl with a really nice voice.” An idea however for some SM children who are not 'too anxious' (determined with clinician’s guidance) would be to tape their voice on a tape recorder for a few classmates to hear. Believe it or not, there are many SM children who will be fine with this idea! Your child may or may not want to be present when the tape is heard. You need to honor her decision on the taping. If she is completely against taping, do not push! Pushing the issue will only cause more anxiety.

I have a 5 year old. She is selectively mute at school but we are making some breakthroughs. Another concern is that she is quite difficult at home. Is this together with Selective Mutism, or is it another problem separate from anxiety of speaking in certain situations? Glad to hear your child is making breakthroughs at school. This is positive! Regarding negative behaviors: YES, this is quite common, and more the norm for many of our anxious children. Unlike adults who should have the maturity and experience to deal with a situation, our young SM children often do not. They therefore act out behaviorally out of frustration. Something as easy as SPEAKING to most people is virtually impossible for them. This is debilitating and frustrating. Although your child is making strides, I assume she is still working through her anxiety. Perhaps the times your child is acting out the most, there is an association to a stressful social situation or feeling out of control feeling a loss of control. Characteristic of our children is their inner need for control. This is common with all anxieties...not just SM. Therefore GIVING your child control should help her feel less anxious and more secure. Much of this control is in the form of helping her to acknowledge and assess her feelings. This means helping her to express and gain control of her feelings. Your treatment professional should have some tactics to help your child begin and master this process. NOTE: Children with sensory processing challenges may have emotional regulation challenges, such as sensory processing challenges which lead to behavioral difficulties. Parenting skills to allow for consistency, predictability and routine are needed to help our behaviorally challenged youngsters.

My 7 yr old daughter has SM. However, my husband does not want to talk to her about her mutism. He agrees she needs help, but does not want the Doctor or anyone else to talk to her about her mutism. He finally agreed on using medication, but again, does not want to tell our daughter about WHY she is taking medication. I do not agree with this approach. I believe she needs to know what is going on. Any advice? YES - you are correct. How can a person begin to overcome something if he/she does not know what they are overcoming? Your child MUST know why she is going for help. She needs to understand her feelings of anxiety. Tactics and techniques are needed to help your child progress. Your daughter needs to be an active participant in her treatment plan. She needs to feel in control of her feelings. Knowledge = Control. 
Regarding medication, this is an individual approach. Some parents are set against telling their child he/she is taking medication to help relieve anxiety, while other parents are very open and hold nothing back. Obviously, the older the child, the more curious he/she will be! For some parents, explaining to the child that he/she needs to take a vitamin is a simple and acceptable approach for many. Other children will demand more explanations. Parents are often reluctant to tell their children about medication for three main reasons: 
a. They do not want their child to THINK he/she is sick or ill. Medication is something you take when you are ill. For parents that are comfortable with using the ’medication’ term, it is explained to the child that the medication will help them feel less anxious. It should be noted that parents should never ever tell a child the medication will ‘help him/ her speak.’ Telling this to a child will just cause resistance. To the child, talking = fear! 
b. Parents worry that the child will become dependent on the medication. Fortunately, this does not happen. The common medications, such as the SSRI’s, are not habit forming...and since the children are not being told the medication will ‘make them speak’ they do not see the medication as magic bullet. 
c. The parents are seeing the medication from a stigma point of view. SSRI’s, the most common medications used to help our children, are ANTI-DEPRESSANTS also. This is not something that many parents handle very well. They often feel guilty, insecure and often embarrassed that THEIR pride and joy is taking a ‘mental drug.’ Unfortunately, this thought process only hurts the child. If a parent learnt as much as possible about these medications, they would see that their insecurity is based on their lack of understanding and knowledge of the medications. If a child had diabetes, most would not hesitate to give them insulin. If a child had strept throat, most would give antibiotics. In other words, these medications WORK, and when monitored correctly, they have minimal side effects. And since goals for medication are 9-12 months, the use is very short term

我7岁的女儿有SM。不过，我丈夫不想跟她讲她的缄默。他同意她需要帮助，但不希望医生或任何其他人与她谈论她的缄默。他最终同意使用药物，但又不想告诉我们的女儿为什么她正在服用药物。我不同意这种做法。我相信她需要知道发生了什么。有什么建议？

关于药物治疗，这是一种个性化处理方法。有些家长反对告诉他们的孩子他/她正在服药以缓解焦虑，而其他父母则非常开放，毫无保留。显然，孩子越大，他/她会越好奇！对于一些父母来说，向孩子解释他/她需要服用维生素对许多人来说是一种简单而可接受的方法。其他孩子会要求更多的解释。父母通常不愿意告诉他们的孩子使用药物，主要有三个原因：

B 父母担心孩子会依赖药物。幸运的是，这并没有发生。常见的药物，如SSRI's，并不是习惯形成......并且由于没有告诉孩子药物会“让他们说话”，他们并不认为药物是魔法子弹。 
C 父母从耻辱的角度看待药物。 SSRI是用于帮助我们孩子的最常用的药物，也是抗抑郁药。这不是许多父母能处理得很好的事情。他们常常感到内疚，没有安全感，常常感到尴尬，他们的骄傲和快乐正在服用“精神药物”。不幸的是，这种思维过程只会伤害孩子。如果父母尽可能多地了解这些药物，他们会发现他们的不安全性是基于他们对药物缺乏了解和知识。如果一个孩子患有糖尿病，大多数人会毫不犹豫地给他们胰岛素。如果一个孩子患上了咽喉炎，大多数人会服用抗生素。换句话说，这些药物是有效的，当正确监测时，它们的副作用很小。由于用药目标是9-12个月，所以使用时间很短。
